About Me

I first realized my interest in electronics by playing Microsoft Flight Simulator (MFS) in high school. The funny part about MFS is how true to life it is: to fly an Airbus A320neo (my favorite plane in MFS), you have to spend 90 minutes calibrating the flight computer hardware before you even think about taxiing out to the runway. Experience

Institute for Space & Defense Electronics (ISDE)

Radiation Effects Intern
Nashville, TN | Summer 2026

During my summer at the Institute for Space & Defense Electronics (ISDE), I was tasked with engineering a high-voltage evaluation platform to characterize the circuit-level effect of radiation degradation in Silicon Carbide (SiC) power devices. I began by modeling DC-DC buck converters in LTSpice using SiC device SPICE models from Rohm and Wolfspeed, tuning RLC parameters and evaluating topological variations to inform a physical hardware design. Additionally, I built an LTSpice model to simulate general radiation damage by evaluating circuit performance shifts caused by varying threshold voltage (Vt) and other basic parameters.

However, transitioning from software simulation to physical hardware presented intense debugging challenges. I encountered continuous hardware faults and impossible gate voltages. To isolate the root cause, I systematically broke the system down, pivoting from full converter operation to double pulse testing on individual MOSFETs. This diagnostic approach allowed me to discover a subtle gate-driver grounding mismatch between 3-terminal and 4-terminal device packages. After correcting the grounding, I successfully achieved a working Low-Side double pulse test, with experimental VDS and ID waveforms perfectly matching my theoretical models. I also designed a PCB accessory board to switch between different 3-terminal device pinouts to improve future project workflows.

Finally, when attempting full buck converter operation, I ran into a clock signal ringing caused by parasitic inductance, which propagated to the high-side gate and triggered the driver's protective fault state. I concluded my internship by documenting these high-frequency issues and delivering a corrective hardware roadmap as my primary deliverable. This roadmap outlined circuit layout improvements (such as physical ground separation and connection optimization strategies) to mitigate interference and guide the next iteration of the lab's testing platform.

Basis

Electrical Engineering Intern
Christchurch, NZ | Fall 2026 - Present

While studying abroad in New Zealand, I interned at Basis, a smart home technology startup. I was tasked with prototyping a real-time energy monitoring system for their smart circuit breaker products. The core engineering challenge was Non-Intrusive Load Monitoring (NILM): taking aggregated household electrical data and disaggregating it to identify the power consumption of individual appliances. This system would therefore allow appliance-level tracking of power usage.

To solve this, I trained a TransUNet-based deep learning model capable of isolating complex appliance power signatures from noisy household datasets. I established a robust data processing pipeline using open-source NILM datasets and optimized the model's architecture. My final model achieved a 92% turn-on event prediction accuracy, outperforming current state-of-the-art NILM research literature. This algorithm established the foundational prototype for eventual firmware integration into the next generation of Basis' smart circuit breakers.

Model Performance (Washing Machine & Microwave): The dotted blue line represents the raw, noisy aggregate household data fed into the model. The black line is the true ground-truth power draw of the specific appliance, while the red line is the model's prediction. As shown, the TransUNet model successfully ignores the background noise and highly accurately tracks the true power signature of the individual appliances.

Aerospace Projects

Rocket Team (Vanderbilt Aerospace Design Laboratory)

Hardware, Avionics, & Outreach
Nashville, TN | 2025 - Present

As a member of the Hardware and Avionics sub-teams for the Vanderbilt Aerospace Design Laboratory (VADL), I compete in the NASA Student Launch Competition as part of an interdisciplinary engineering team. I was heavily involved in the integration, testing, and debugging of both hardware and software across our avionics and mission-critical systems.

By rigorously analyzing our payload requirements, I architected a sensor replacement that cut our avionics budget by $1,300 while simultaneously improving flight data accuracy. Furthermore, when our Parachute Detachment System encountered a critical hardware latch fault, I diagnosed the issue and helped implement a moving average filter in software to smooth noisy altimeter data. These mitigations ensured 100% parachute deployment reliability across all our field tests.

I also co-authored highly detailed Preliminary, Critical, and Flight Readiness Review (PDR/CDR/FRR) technical reports in LaTeX for NASA engineer judges, and co-led numerous STEM outreach events to bring aerospace engineering concepts to local K-12 students.
